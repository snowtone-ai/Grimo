# Grimo — Partner Experience Motion & Anatomy Evidence

---

# 1. Product Goal

The original research evaluated a living-companion experience characterized by the following:

- is unmistakably the selected character;
- appears alive before user input;
- appears to notice the user and the contacted body region;
- reacts with readable bodily causality;
- can hold, wait, hesitate, invite, recover, and retain emotion;
- preserves weight and support instead of floating;
- remains convincing for sessions of roughly 30 seconds to 5 minutes;
- avoids collapsing into one canned reaction loop.

The experience criteria documented in the source include cuteness, comfort, attachment, fun, surprise, collection, local touch response, gaze/face response, whole-body follow-through, secondary motion, interruption, variation, and emotional afterglow.

The experience dimensions considered in this historical research were:

`IDENTITY + CAUSALITY + ATTENTION + WEIGHT + SPATIAL BELIEVABILITY + VARIATION + CONTINUITY`

# 2. Corpus and Why These Five Videos

Only the five specified MP4s were used as primary architecture evidence.

| ID | Video | Architecture question |
|---|---|---|
| E1 | Eevee V02 — high-five | Can one forelimb move toward the user while the rest of a quadruped remains grounded and quiet? |
| E2 | Eevee V03 — evolution stone | What surfaces and ordering changes are actually exposed by side/profile → front reorientation? |
| E3 | Eevee V06 — cheek rub | How much head/body commitment, compression, rebound, and forepaw participation is required for sustained positive touch? |
| P1 | Pikachu V2 — anger / rejection | What is the maximum useful non-neutral orientation/deformation envelope, including withdrawal and side/back states? |
| P2 | Pikachu V6 — cheek rub | Which interaction mechanics survive a very different body morphology? |

The existing Master Inventories and detailed video analyses were used only to locate architecture-relevant windows. The actual MP4s were then inspected for depth, self-occlusion, surface exposure, support, apparent COM behavior, and front/back ordering.

Eevee V02 already provides sufficient evidence that a presented forepaw is not merely a flat local facial event: the paw rotates to show the pad, occupies a foreground layer, changes overlap against the head/ruff, and is held toward the player while the rest of the body stays comparatively stable.

# 3. Architecture-Relevant Episodes

Evidence labels:

- **[V1]** direct observation from the supplied MP4 frames.
- **[V2]** temporal/spatial inference from adjacent frames.
- **[I]** architecture inference.

## E1 — Eevee high-five

### E1-A — Paw offer / WAIT / contact / acknowledgement

**Representative window:** ~33.23–34.77 s; repeated offers begin ~35.23, 37.77, 41.27 s.

**User perception:** Eevee intentionally offers one paw, waits, receives contact, and acknowledges success.

**Visible evidence [V1]:**

- one forepaw rises from the chest/support region and presents its pad toward camera;
- the paw becomes a foreground object and overlaps the face/ruff differently through the phrase;
- the paw orientation changes enough for the palm/pad to become the dominant visible surface;
- opposite forelimb, hind support, torso, and tail remain comparatively quiet;
- after contact, facial acknowledgement occurs without requiring a large root translation.

**Spatial implication [I]:** a forelimb requires independent forward depth excursion, orientation, and contact ownership while the support base remains mostly stable.

**3D necessity:** **D3 (spatial-analysis classification).** A single fixed 2D animation could fake this exact pose, but a reusable left/right, interruptible, touch-local, prop-compatible system would require view/perspective asset multiplication and manual layer-order management.

---

## E2 — Eevee side/profile state and return

### E2-A — Side/profile hold

**Representative frame:** ~30.90 s.

**User perception:** the same character exists in profile, not as a front sprite rotated flat.

**Visible evidence [V1]:**

- one eye is dominant while the far eye disappears through true facial self-occlusion;
- muzzle/forehead depth becomes visible;
- near/far ear ordering changes;
- ruff, torso, tail, and rear body create layered depth relationships impossible to infer from the front silhouette alone.

**3D necessity:** **D3.** The visible surface set itself changes.

### E2-B — Side → front reorientation

**Representative transition:** ~42.3–43.6 s; Master representative timestamp ~42.90 s.

**Visible evidence [V1]:**

- the head leads into the turn;
- the visible eye set changes from profile to frontal bilateral read;
- ears exchange depth/order;
- tail and torso pass through 3/4 overlap states;
- the character reveals new front surfaces continuously rather than cutting between unrelated views.

**3D necessity:** **D3.** Articulated 2.5D would require view-specific art, swap logic, and layer-order transitions precisely where interruption and continuity matter.

---

## E3 — Eevee cheek-rub / deep positive touch

### E3-A — Deep bow → rebound → paw participation

**Representative windows:** ~20.10–22.60 s and ~65.90–68.60 s.

**User perception:** Eevee accepts sustained touch, commits its body into it, compresses, rebounds, and adds a positive forepaw/face payoff.

**Visible evidence [V1]:**

- head moves deeply downward, exposing the top of the head and changing the visible head/ruff transition;
- ears rotate and follow rather than moving as a rigid whole with the skull;
- face can become partially or almost completely hidden at the deepest bow;
- rebound reveals forepaws as the upper body returns;
- support remains largely grounded; the action reads as a body commitment around planted support, not a floating jump.

**3D necessity:** **D3 for a reusable interaction system.** A front-only 2D morph could imitate one bow, but the changing exposed surfaces, occlusion, bilateral touch locality, and interruptible rebound make spatial 3D materially more robust.

## P1 — Pikachu negative / withdrawal envelope

### P1-A — Face-led recoil / compression

**Representative window:** ~32.85–34.55 s.

**User perception:** discomfort begins locally and escalates into body withdrawal.

**Visible evidence [V1]:**

- face changes before the largest body change;
- head/upper torso compress downward/laterally;
- forelimbs and ears follow;
- hind support remains comparatively stable.

**3D necessity:** **D2–D3.** The reaction could be approximated in 2D, but spatial head/torso withdrawal and support preservation strongly benefit from 3D.

### P1-B — Large compression → expansion

**Representative window:** ~48.60–49.57 s.

**Visible evidence [V1]:** the body visibly compresses, then expands upward/outward with forelimb participation while the lower support remains grounded.

**3D necessity:** **D3** for an architecture expected to support comparable large reactions and interruption.

### P1-C — Side/back disengagement and glance-back

**Representative windows:** glance ~58.55–59.50 s; disengaged side/back hold ~59.50–63.95 s.

**Visible evidence [V1]:**

- front facial information becomes intentionally unavailable;
- rear/side torso and tail become the dominant silhouette;
- head can glance back without the whole body returning front;
- the body can remain in a meaningful non-front moving hold.

**3D necessity:** **D3** for this exact behavior family.

### P1-D — Side/back → front return

**Representative window:** ~67.20–68.15 s.

**Visible evidence [V1]:** head, torso/root, ears, tail, and support orientation re-establish front-facing presentation through a continuous spatial transition.

**3D necessity:** **D3.** This is a true orientation transition, not a local facial state.

## P2 — Pikachu positive touch on different morphology

### P2-A — Large positive whole-body delight

**Representative window:** ~19.90–20.60 s.

**Visible evidence [V1]:** upper body rises/expands, forelimbs open, face peaks, tail/ears follow; support remains readable.

**3D necessity:** **D2–D3.** It can be pre-authored in 2D, but interaction continuity and body support benefit strongly from spatial 3D.

### P2-B — Deep lateral content lean + ear rotation

**Representative windows:** ~32.70–34.30 s and ~62.40–64.10 s.

**Visible evidence [V1]:**

- head leads laterally toward contact;
- torso and COM follow;
- feet remain planted;
- ear orientation changes in depth;
- the character later recenters.

**3D necessity:** **D3** for bilateral/local touch because the contacted side must change the direction of depth, occlusion, and lean.

### P2-C — Bilateral cheek-level ecstatic stretch

**Representative window:** ~41.20–43.90 s.

**Visible evidence [V1]:** both forelimbs rise around the face, head/chest expand, then the body returns through a long settle.

**3D necessity:** **D2–D3.** The important lesson is channel recruitment and support, not exact pose copying.

# 4. What Actually Creates the Sense of Life

Across both species, the strongest architecture-relevant principles are:

1. **Selective channel activity.** A paw, face, ear, or head can act while the rest of the body remains quiet.
2. **Local causality.** Contact is acknowledged at the touched region before or alongside larger emotion.
3. **Stable support under expressive upper-body action.** Large emotion does not require constant stepping or root bouncing.
4. **Spatial commitment when emotion becomes large.** Head/torso/COM eventually commit; the character does not remain a facial puppet.
5. **Asymmetric appendage freedom.** Left/right ears and forelimbs exhibit separately readable actions.
6. **Intentional stillness and WAIT.** The ability to hold a meaningful pose is as important as animation amplitude.
7. **Recovery and afterglow.** The character settles through intermediate states rather than snapping neutral.
8. **Orientation continuity.** Profile/3/4/front transitions reveal a persistent body with coherent depth.

The sense of life is therefore not proportional to mesh complexity. It is proportional to **semantic control + readable cause + support + timing + state continuity**.

# 6. Counterfactual 2D / 2.5D Analysis

## What articulated 2.5D could do well

A sophisticated 2.5D system could reproduce:

- blinks;
- small facial expression changes;
- shallow head tilts;
- minor ear flicks;
- restrained front-view breathing/posture drift;
- several fixed front-view positive reactions.

If Grimo were permanently front-only, never presented a limb toward the player, never turned profile, never used spatial props, and tolerated view-specific authored assets, 2.5D would remain credible.

## Where it begins to fail economically or visually

### 1. Forward paw presentation

A 2.5D system needs dedicated paw perspective art, depth/layer ordering against face and fleece, side-specific variants, and interruption handling. A true 3D limb obtains those relationships continuously.

### 2. Side/profile → front continuity

2.5D needs either discrete view swaps or a dense deformable view interpolation system. The visible eye set, ear ordering, tail ordering, and torso surfaces change continuously in E2.

### 3. Bilateral local touch

Left and right cheek interactions must lean in opposite spatial directions and preserve contact locality. 2.5D doubles or multiplies pose assets and creates edge cases when input interrupts between views.

### 4. Future Food / Wear / props / Secret Motion

Spatial mouth/hoof/ear/fleece anchors, occlusion, and prop handoff become increasingly expensive as screen-space layers and bespoke artwork.

### 5. Authoring growth

The problem is not whether one 2.5D clip can look good. It can. The problem is whether *dozens of interruptible, side-aware, prop-aware, state-dependent behaviors* remain manageable without an asset/view explosion.

# 8. Surface Exposure Inferred from the Motion Examples

## Regions that become exposed during motion

The study identified these surfaces as potentially visible during head, limb and body movement:

- underside of jaw / lower cheek;
- lower head and concealed head/chest transition;
- upper chest immediately below the face/fleece opening;
- shoulder / forelimb root regions during forehoof offer;
- side eye/socket surface beyond the strict front view;
- rear/inner ear-root surfaces revealed by yaw;
- limited upper abdomen/belly surface revealed during bow/lean;
- tail root/tuft attachment region if the fleece parts enough to reveal it;

## Regions normally occluded in the studied poses

The study identified the following regions as largely hidden under the examined interaction conditions:

- deep central chassis interior;
- hidden chest volume behind fleece;
- most abdomen surface under fleece;
- pelvis/support core;
- most rear torso under fleece;
- deep limb roots hidden by fleece;
- internal neck/chest bridge not visible in the examined poses;
- internal collision/support proxy surfaces;
- topology whose only role is skinning continuity.

# 9. Grounded Support and Center-of-Mass Motion Patterns

The five videos consistently show that upper-body expressiveness does not require continuous large locomotion.

# 10. Cross-Species Motion Findings

Eevee V06 and Pikachu V6 have very different morphology, yet the following mechanics survive:

- local contact or evaluation precedes large body commitment;
- face communicates social meaning early;
- head/body can lean into positive contact;
- support stays grounded longer than the emotional upper body;
- ears/appendages follow asymmetrically or with delay;
- forelimbs can add acknowledgement without taking over every reaction;
- larger emotion recruits more channels;
- recovery is staged rather than an instant reset.

These observations support common behavior/weight/timing patterns while leaving species-specific performance open.

What does **not** transfer directly:

- exact ear trajectory;
- exact tail behavior;
- exact forelimb pose;
- exact compression amount;
- exact head/body ratio;
- exact reaction timing.

### Candidate inspection questions

For each state ask only:

2. Does the cause of the pose read?
3. Does the body feel supported?
4. Does the relevant depth/occlusion behave spatially?
5. Are exposed surfaces acceptable at the fidelity class assigned to them?
6. Can the pose blend/recover without an obvious break?

# 12. Unresolved Physical / Motion Questions

The 2026-09-23 source identified the following questions as unresolved; none is treated here as a new production task:

- exact 3/4 socket construction required by the canonical eye shape;
- how much under-jaw/head→chest surface becomes visible once fleece is present;
- how much the fleece can hide/support modular underbody seams during motion;
- minimum torso deformation needed for one-forehoof support transfer;
- exact lateral/forward COM shifts that feel heavy but cute;
- amount of fleece regional deformation before the cloud identity becomes jelly-like;
- safe tail-root exposure and tail motion amplitude;
- exact mouth/cheek envelope for future feeding;
- final Wear/prop clearances under head yaw and forehoof offers;
